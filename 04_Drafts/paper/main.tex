\section{Introduction}
Real-time rendering must balance image quality against the time available to
produce each frame. Rendering at a lower resolution and reconstructing a
higher-resolution image can reduce rendering work, but the reconstruction
itself consumes time and must deal with aliased or missing detail
\cite{xiao2020neural,mercier2023efficient}. Temporal reconstruction reuses
samples from previous frames, while neural methods can combine learned image
features with information supplied by the renderer. Both approaches must
handle motion and avoid introducing visible artifacts.

Rendering buffers offer geometric and temporal cues beyond color. Prior
work shows how these cues can assist reconstruction: Xiao et al.\ combine
depth and motion-aligned history in a neural system, while Wen et al.\ study
rendering inputs as part of an architecture-optimization framework
\cite{xiao2020neural,wen2025cgsr}. Mercier et al.\ compare temporal
reconstruction with and without warping and show why image-wide scores can
miss thin-object artifacts \cite{mercier2023efficient}. These findings
identify useful inputs and evaluation concerns, but their effects depend on
the model, rendering setup, and processing used for each comparison.

We propose a controlled study of auxiliary inputs for a lightweight neural
upscaler using Mercier et al.'s QRISP dataset
\cite{mercier2023efficient}. Our primary comparisons isolate depth and
motion-alignment effects on matched current-frame and single-history
inputs, with reconstruction quality, runtime, memory, and scene-level
variation evaluated under one protocol. Surface-normal comparisons remain
conditional because QRISP does not document a surface-normal modality.
This is not a claim that auxiliary-input or alignment ablations are new;
the model and data checks remain to be validated experimentally.

\section{Background and Related Work}
\subsection{Efficient Neural Reconstruction}
Real-time reconstruction must recover detail within a frame-time budget.
Xiao et al.\ show that a neural supersampler can use renderer-provided depth
and motion vectors to combine current color with reprojected features from
previous frames \cite{xiao2020neural}. Its architecture and combined input
changes, however, do not isolate the effect of each cue. Mercier et
al.\ develop a more efficient temporal network and a rendered gaming dataset;
their model-size and component studies connect reconstruction quality to
inference cost and the workload being measured \cite{mercier2023efficient}.
Wen et al.\ instead integrate rendering properties into network construction,
architecture search, and pruning across super-resolution backbones
\cite{wen2025cgsr}. Their optimization demonstrates that input choice and
model computation can change together. These approaches motivate studying
input choice in relation to the cost of reconstruction, rather than treating
quality differences as properties of the inputs alone.

\subsection{Geometric Guidance}
Depth indicates scene distance, while surface normals indicate orientation;
they may therefore supply different cues at edges and on changing surfaces.
Wen et al.\ sequentially add depth, normal direction, and derived edge
information in CGSR, relating those properties to reconstruction errors and
model size \cite{wen2025cgsr}. This is an existing rendering-input ablation,
but its sequential design and network optimization couple input choice to
changes in capacity.

Zheng et al.\ place normals and material information in a detail-reconstruction
module, while depth and motion constrain temporal feature refinement
\cite{zheng2025rdg}. Their ablations compare decoupled guidance against
concatenation and alternative fusion modules. They show that the way signals
are combined matters, rather than isolating each buffer's contribution.
Together, these studies motivate examining depth and normals individually
and together while distinguishing input choice from fusion design.

\subsection{Temporal History, Alignment, and Failure Cases}
Temporal history can supply samples missing from the current frame, provided
they correspond to the current view. Karis's temporal antialiasing method
uses motion vectors to reproject history and constrains incompatible history
with a current-frame neighborhood \cite{karis2014high}. It also describes
failure at silhouettes and where motion is inaccurate. These principles
inform our interest in aligned history without implying adoption of the
complete antialiasing method.

Xiao et al.\ compare single-frame and temporal reconstruction, and study
alternative reprojection settings \cite{xiao2020neural}. Their separate
depth-and-motion-input comparison also replaces estimated optical flow with
renderer motion vectors, so it does not isolate either change. Mercier et
al.\ explicitly compare their temporal model with no warping and with
estimated flow \cite{mercier2023efficient}. Their results do not determine
the effect in a different lightweight model and rendering setup.

Both works also caution against relying only on whole-image averages. Xiao
et al.\ show disocclusion ghosting when feature reweighting is removed
\cite{xiao2020neural}. In Mercier et al.'s 2$\times$ medium-model ablation,
removing depth-informed motion-vector dilation improves aggregate PSNR and
LPIPS, although their thin-object example favors dilation
\cite{mercier2023efficient}. Such cases make localized artifacts relevant to
interpreting reconstruction quality, even when aggregate scores improve.

Taken together, these studies motivate examining renderer-provided geometry
and temporal history in relation to both reconstruction quality and
computational cost. Prior work already includes input and alignment
ablations, but their effects depend on model design, fusion, and rendering
conditions. Our study aims to clarify these trade-offs in a selected
lightweight model and rendering setup.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}
This section specifies a \emph{proposed} reproducible protocol, to be
confirmed by a small dataset-inspection and training pilot before final runs.
No model has yet been trained and no performance results have been collected.
The primary study uses the Qualcomm Rasterized Images for Super-resolution
Processing (QRISP) dataset introduced by Mercier et al.\
\cite{mercier2023efficient}. We distinguish comparisons supported by its
documented modalities from extensions requiring new data.

\subsection{Dataset, Resolution, and Splits}
\paragraph{Source and sampling.}
QRISP contains 8,760 frames from 13 rendered scenes, captured as 60-fps
sequences: 7,260 frames in ten development scenes and 1,500 frames in three
published test scenes. Its modalities are low-resolution color, depth, motion
vectors, and metadata describing camera parameters and per-frame jitter;
surface normals are \emph{not} among the documented modalities
\cite{mercier2023efficient}. The official distribution, supplement, and
research-use license will be retained with the experiment record; availability
of individual files must be verified after download.
The primary proposed experiment is 2$\times$ upscaling from
$960\times540$ to $1920\times1080$. We pair the \texttt{MipBiasMinus1}
non-jittered 540p color render and matching 540p depth/motion captures
with the dataset's \texttt{Enhanced} 1080p color reference. Unlike a
synthetically downsampled input, this is a native low-resolution render.
The enhanced reference was constructed by downsampling a 2160p
render with 8$\times$ MSAA \cite{mercier2023efficient}.
A separate, predeclared experiment may examine the corresponding
\texttt{Jittered} modalities and camera offsets; no jitter comparison is
included in the primary variant numbers below.

\paragraph{Scene separation.}
We will preserve the dataset's three published evaluation scenes as a
completely untouched test partition. Before tuning, two of the ten
development scenes will be designated for validation; the remainder will
be used for training. Scene choice will be documented with a manifest and
checked for overlapping scene families or shared assets. Sequences and
frames from a validation or test scene will not enter training, model
selection, or data-driven preprocessing. If a reduced pilot subset is used,
it will be labeled \emph{pilot only}; final comparisons require an approved,
fixed split. Scene-disjoint evaluation on this dataset does not establish
generalization to all rendering engines.

\paragraph{Integrity and preprocessing.}
The supplementary dataset description specifies 8-bit RGBA PNG color,
32-bit depth values packed into four 8-bit PNG channels, and two-channel
16-bit EXR motion vectors. The documented packed-depth reconstruction is
$D=R/255+G/255^2+B/255^3+A/255^4$, using the unsigned byte
channel values. Motion is stored as vertical velocity followed by
horizontal velocity, normalized to $[-1,1]$; conversion to pixel
displacement requires the appropriate image dimensions and a validated
vertical-axis sign. All modality paths, channel order, numerical ranges,
frame indices, and camera metadata will be checked empirically on a
downloaded sample before use. Depth normalization and invalid-pixel
handling will be fixed using training-set rules only, and the alpha channel
will not silently be treated as a depth or validity mask. LR color and HR
reference RGB will use a single documented color representation and
normalization, identical across variants. Sequence boundaries are
preserved: no history may cross a segment boundary. The first frame of
each segment is excluded from the primary paired temporal comparison so
all models are evaluated on exactly the same eligible frames.

\subsection{Ablation Design}
\label{sec:variants}
Every learned variant receives current low-resolution RGB. The optional
geometry is \emph{current-frame} depth (D) or surface normals (N).
History (H) denotes the immediately preceding low-resolution RGB frame:
either fed without warping (U) or resampled into current-frame coordinates
using the current motion field (A). The network receives \emph{warped
history color}, not raw motion vectors as an additional learned input.
All temporal variants use precisely one previous frame; they do not
maintain recurrent predicted features. This isolates alignment more
directly than changing the temporal architecture at the same time.

The six \emph{core} variants V0, V1, V4--V7 allow a depth contrast
(V1--V0); a history contrast (V4--V0); alignment contrasts without depth
(V5--V4) and with depth (V7--V6); and an alignment-by-depth
interaction, $(V7-V6)-(V5-V4)$, with differences calculated on a
predefined quality scale. The six conditional normal variants complete
the four geometric combinations crossed with both history conditions.
We will not claim to test independent surface normals on QRISP unless
real renderer-provided normals become available: normals reconstructed
from depth would instead be an explicitly labeled \emph{derived-depth
feature} control, not an independent-normal experiment.
The previous V6 designation (depth plus normals with aligned history)
is replaced by the systematic numbering in Table~\ref{tab:variants};
the updated experiment log must use these IDs consistently.

\paragraph{Jitter, motion, and learned features.}
Jitter is a \emph{capture condition}, not an extra auxiliary modality in
the primary non-jittered matrix. If the optional jittered condition is
added, the same variants will be repeated on matched jittered sequences,
with camera offsets available to all variants and explicit jitter
compensation in warping; results will be stratified by condition rather
than mixed into the main estimates. Mipmap bias and target construction
must remain fixed within each comparison. Predicted recurrent features
are \emph{not} part of this architecture; adding them would be a
separate architecture ablation rather than a simple feature column.
Depth-informed motion-vector dilation is also omitted from the main
alignment comparison because including it only for one treatment would
confound the effect of warping.

\paragraph{Hypotheses.}
The following are testable expectations, not findings.
\begin{description}
\item[H1] Adding depth improves single-frame quality (V1 versus V0).
\item[H2] If independent normals are available, normals improve
single-frame quality (V2 versus V0).
\item[H3] If normals are available, depth and normals yield a
subadditive gain in the selected quality metric:
$(V3-V0)<(V1-V0)+(V2-V0)$. This is a metric-scale
interaction, not proof of information-theoretic redundancy.
\item[H4] Motion alignment improves history-based reconstruction,
both without depth (V5 versus V4) and with depth (V7 versus V6).
\item[H5] Differences persist, subject to uncertainty, on the
held-out scenes rather than being confined to training scenes.
\end{description}

\subsection{Neural Architecture and Temporal Processing}
A small fully convolutional residual CNN is proposed for every learned
variant, with 32 hidden feature channels, an initial $3\times3$
convolution, four two-convolution residual blocks, and a final
convolution yielding 12 channels followed by
\texttt{PixelShuffle(2)}. Its predicted HR residual is added to
bilinear-upsampled current RGB. The network operates chiefly at LR
resolution. To control nominal parameter count, each variant uses the
same input tensor slots: current RGB (3), depth (1), normals (3), and
history RGB (3), totaling ten channels. Absent modalities are zero-filled
under a fixed representation, and a \emph{separate model is trained from
scratch for each variant}. Active parameter use still varies by input,
so model size, active channels, computational cost, and latency will all
be reported.

For V5, V7, V9, and V11, the previous LR color is warped with bilinear
sampling using current-frame motion vectors. The motion-vector channel
order, normalized-to-pixel conversion, current-to-previous sign,
sampling-coordinate convention, border handling, and any occlusion mask
will be established using numerical warp checks and recorded. For
V4, V6, V8, and V10, the \emph{same} previous LR color is supplied
without warping. Warping is deterministic and does not change the
learned network; the input size and the number of historical frames are
the same for matched aligned and unaligned models. Any validity mask
introduced for aligned history must also be provided identically to
its unaligned counterpart or excluded from this primary contrast.
This design does not evaluate the recursive feature-and-output history
used by Mercier et al.'s complete temporal system
\cite{mercier2023efficient}.

\subsection{Training and Reproducibility}
Training will use aligned $64\times64$ LR patches and corresponding
$128\times128$ HR target crops. For temporal inputs, motion-based warping
will be computed in full-frame coordinates \emph{before} extracting
corresponding crops, avoiding artificial displacement discontinuities
at patch boundaries. A common random sampling manifest will
identify eligible scenes, segments, and crop coordinates for all
variants, with patch origins adjusted consistently for any optional
jitter condition. Training uses an L1 loss in the fixed RGB
representation, AdamW, an initial learning rate of $10^{-3}$, and a
provisional batch size of eight. The proposed full-run budget is 20,000
optimizer updates per variant with validation every 1,000 updates,
selecting a checkpoint by validation PSNR; these numbers will be
revised \emph{once, before freezing the protocol}, if a measured
end-to-end pilot shows that they are not feasible on the 8-GB RTX 4060.
Fixed-budget comparison is preferred to training each variant for
different amounts of wall-clock time. Mixed precision may be used if
numerical and repeatability checks pass. Random cropping is the
default augmentation; spatial flips/rotations will not be used unless
the corresponding motion-vector and camera-coordinate transforms are
implemented and tested. All full comparisons will be repeated with
three recorded random seeds.

Each run will record the Git revision, dataset version and checksum,
exact scene/segment manifest, input condition, crop sampling policy,
model configuration, optimizer, schedule, seed, package versions,
hardware, checkpoint path, and validation history. No test images
will be used for hyperparameter selection. Reported experiments may
start only after dataset formats and the split are checked and both
authors approve the frozen protocol. No dataset download, completed
run, or training result is asserted here.

\subsection{Evaluation and Statistical Analysis}
\label{sec:metrics}
Our primary fidelity measure will be RGB PSNR evaluated per frame on
normalized $[0,1]$ predictions and references, with peak value one:
$\mathrm{PSNR}=10\log_{10}(1/\mathrm{MSE})$. PSNR will be computed for
each frame before averaging, not computed from a pooled MSE. SSIM will
be a complementary image-structure measure \cite{wang2004ssim};
its implementation, version, Gaussian window, channel order, and
\texttt{data\_range=1} will be fixed in code before test evaluation.
We will use exactly the same frame identifiers, spatial crop, channel
policy, invalid-pixel mask, and color encoding for all variants.
Per-frame results will first be summarized within each scene and
seed; primary summaries will weight held-out scenes equally and show
their individual scores and paired differences. We will show
seed-to-seed variation and scene-level uncertainty without treating
adjacent frames as independent samples. With only three test scenes,
any interval or significance claim will be qualified accordingly.

Bicubic interpolation of the same LR input will be a non-neural
reference. For spatial error analysis, LDR-FLIP is a candidate on
consistently prepared display-referred images
\cite{andersson2020flip}; HDR-FLIP is not appropriate for the
documented 8-bit RGB samples without a separate HDR image pipeline
\cite{andersson2021hdrflip}. LPIPS remains an optional complementary
metric \cite{zhang2018lpips}, not evidence of human preference.
Visual crops, synchronized output/reference sequences, and error
maps will document both improvements and failures. Static-segment
temporal variation may be measured where verified stationary
segments exist; motion-compensated temporal errors require independent
visibility/correspondence checks. Framewise image metrics alone do
not establish temporal stability.

We will report parameters, peak measured GPU memory, and warmed-up
batch-one inference time at the full 1080p output size using CUDA
events and synchronization. Timing will distinguish (i) network-only
execution and (ii) offline GPU pipeline execution including motion
conversion and history warping, while documenting transfers and
other excluded costs. Inference and any warping must be measured on
the same device, with fixed precision, warm-up counts, repetitions,
and memory-allocation procedure. Evaluation metrics and reference
processing are excluded from deployment latency. As this is an
offline PyTorch experiment on an existing Unity-rendered dataset,
we cannot claim measured Unreal Engine integration costs or a
demonstrated real-time frame-rate budget merely from these timings.

\subsection{Feasibility Gates and Limitations}
The first gate is acquiring a small training-scene sample under the
dataset's research license and validating color, packed depth, EXR
motion, camera JSON, corresponding frame IDs, and disk requirements.
The second is an end-to-end baseline and aligned/unaligned warp pilot,
including inspection at motion boundaries. The third is freezing
the scene split, training budget, and metrics before final experiments.
If the published files do not include independent normals, H2--H3
and V2--V3/V8--V11 remain explicitly out of scope for QRISP.
Any later custom-engine normals experiment would constitute a
separate dataset/domain with new controls. Results obtained on QRISP
would be specific to its Unity-rendered scenes and chosen capture
conditions, not direct evidence of performance inside Unreal Engine.
